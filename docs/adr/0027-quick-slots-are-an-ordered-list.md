# Quick slots are an ordered list of pins, without places or a limit

Accepted 2026-10-05 by the user's decision, redesigning the pinned home of #101; the core implements it, and the window's parts landed in [d67ceb5](https://github.com/pane-app/pane/commit/d67ceb57bfa250213a23cc1b4f2c37fdb39cf224). It supersedes the five positional slots of [ADR 0026](0026-host-keeps-quick-slots-by-identity.md), their "replace a slot" step and the first version of their record. ADR 0026's rule that a slot holds an identity, never a row, its resolution through the registry as it is then, and its house record rules stand unchanged.

So the quick slots are an ordered list of pins with no gaps and no length limit. Pinning adds a pin at the end; unpinning removes it and closes the gap; Move Up and Move Down swap a pin with its neighbour, never past either end. Nothing is replaced, so there is no slot to choose, and pinning what is already pinned changes nothing. The Actions panel offers "Pin" on a root search row a slot can hold and "Unpin" on one already pinned; a pin's own panel offers "Unpin", "Move Up" and "Move Down" ("Move Left" and "Move Right" in the horizontal layout). The default keys: Ctrl+Shift+F pins or unpins a focused pin or root search's selected row, and Ctrl+Alt+Up or Left and Ctrl+Alt+Down or Right move the focused pin (Cmd for Ctrl on macOS).

The record `quick-slots.json` is now version 2, `{ "version": 2, "pins": [ { "command": "<id>" }, { "command": "<id>", "result": "<id>" } ] }`, every pin in order and none empty. A version 1 record (five positional `slots`, `null` for an empty one) is read as the list of its pins in order, and the next change writes version 2. A record of an unknown version, or one that is not valid (an entry that is not a pin, an unknown field in one, a target pinned twice), is still reported and never replaced while Pane runs.

Only the first five pins take a number: Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+5 (Cmd on macOS). The numbers after the numbered pins go to the first rows below the pinned home, up to 9. With two pins, Ctrl+3 to Ctrl+9 pick the first seven rows. With eight, Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+5 pick pins 1 to 5, pins 6 to 8 have no number, and Ctrl+6 to Ctrl+9 pick the first four rows. With a query, or in a command's list, Ctrl+1 to Ctrl+9 pick the first rows. The numbers show only while Ctrl is held alone for 400 ms. A pin without a number is still invoked by a click, or by Enter or Space while it has focus.

The Launcher page lays the pins out horizontally (the default) or vertically. Horizontal is a grid of five columns that wraps onto more rows, with one dashed "+ Pin" hint tile in the next free cell of the last row: it never starts a row of its own, so a full last row shows none, and with no pins the hint is all there is. Vertical shows the pins as result rows, and nothing when there are none. The compact window mode shows no pins unless the user turns on "Show pinned in compact window mode" (`compactPinned` in the settings record, off by default). Then the pins show as a row of small icons under the search field while the window is collapsed, and the first five show their numbers while Ctrl is held.

Five fixed places were not kept. The model, Raycast's Favorites, is an unbounded ordered list without positions ([Raycast Favorites](../research/raycast-favorites.md), [Raycast inspiration](../research/raycast-inspiration.md)). Fixed places were meant to keep each pin's number stable, but once the numbering skipped empty slots a hole kept no number in place, so the places had nothing left to give. The user also felt the cap of five was arbitrary.
